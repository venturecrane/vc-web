## Current Draft (v1)

Scott Durgan has spent his career making something from seemingly nothing - cooking, silversmithing, woodworking, software, video production, product management. Different materials, same underlying problem: you need tools and structure before you can do the real work, and nobody's going to hand them to you.

His core skill is analogy. Seeing that the structure of one discipline maps onto another is what lets him move between them. A mise en place is a deployment checklist. A jig is a CI pipeline. The patterns transfer if you're paying attention.

When he set out to build software products as a solo founder, he hit the same problem he always hits: the workshop didn't exist yet. So he built it. Venture Crane is the jigs, the workbench, the tool wall - infrastructure that lets one person direct AI agent teams to build, test, and ship real products. He built the factory before building the products, which is either the most disciplined or the most self-indulgent way to start a venture studio. Probably both.

## Pass 1

### Assessment

**What's working:**

- The opening line is strong. "Making something from seemingly nothing" is the right frame and it's immediately concrete with the list of disciplines.
- The analogy paragraph is the soul of the bio. The mise en place / deployment checklist and jig / CI pipeline comparisons are specific and land well.
- "Probably both" at the end has the right tone - self-aware without being self-deprecating.
- The overall arc (maker identity -> analogy as method -> Venture Crane as workshop) is sound.

**What could be stronger:**

- "Nobody's going to hand them to you" reads slightly generic. It's a sentiment about self-reliance that doesn't add much the reader can't already infer from the list of disciplines.
- "The patterns transfer if you're paying attention" is close to a motivational poster. It tells the reader they should be impressed rather than letting the examples do the work.
- "The jigs, the workbench, the tool wall" - three workshop metaphors in a row after already using "jig" and "mise en place" earlier. The metaphor layer is getting thick.
- The transition from paragraph two to three could be tighter. The reader already understands he builds tools; the third paragraph re-establishes this.

**Why this version:**

1. **Strongest opening rhythm.** "Every time, the same move:" is punchy and sets up the pattern without over-explaining it.
2. **The key insight is now explicit.** "He doesn't experience these as metaphors" is the sentence that separates this bio from a typical "I'm multidisciplinary" founder blurb. It tells the reader that analogy is a cognitive mode, not a writing technique.
3. **Page integration.** Naming the specific methodology elements (session lifecycle, handoff records, quality gates) ties the bio to the content above it. The bio earns its placement on this page rather than being a generic founder section bolted on at the end.
4. **Clean closer.** "Probably depends on how it turns out" is honest about the venture being early-stage without being either falsely humble or falsely confident. It respects the reader.
5. **No fluff.** Every sentence either introduces a fact, makes an argument, or connects to another part of the page. Nothing is there just to sound good.
